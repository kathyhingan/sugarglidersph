## Raised by hand, not by the cage

Every joey here is handled from the moment it is out of pouch. Daily holding, daily voices, daily warmth. By the time a glider is ready to go home, a person is the most normal thing in its world. That is why our joeys settle in days rather than fighting their owners for weeks. It is slow, it limits how many we can raise, and it is the entire point.

## We match, we do not just sell

Before a joey goes anywhere, we ask about your home. A calm joey suits a quiet household. A busy one suits an owner who wants the show. A nervous first-time keeper should not start with the most independent animal in the room. Matching temperament to home is the single biggest factor in whether an owner thrives or gives up, so we treat it as the job, not an afterthought.

## Documented, legal, and yours

Sugar gliders sit under wildlife regulation in the Philippines, and that is a good thing. Every joey we place comes with full DENR paperwork, with ownership recorded in your name. You receive an animal you can keep openly, transport without worry, and never have to explain. A breeder who cannot hand you that documentation is one to walk away from.

## Support that does not end at handover

The first weeks raise the most questions: what is normal, what is not, when to worry. You can reach us through all of it. We would rather answer a dozen small questions in month one than hear about a problem in month six that an early message would have solved.

---

## Why so few at a time

People sometimes ask why we do not raise more. The honest answer is that hand-raising and proper matching do not scale. If we doubled our numbers we would halve the attention each joey gets, and the bonded, people-trusting animal we are known for would stop being either. We would rather place fewer gliders well.
